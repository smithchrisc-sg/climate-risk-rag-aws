Introduction

This solution, QuakeCoRE 2025 Annual Meeting (QCAM25) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Aotearoa New Zealand between University of Canterbury, Public Research Organisations (PROs), Government Ministries and National Agencies, City and Regional Councils, Civil Defence and Emergency Management (CDEM) professionals, Global Earthquake Model (GEM) Foundation, and Industry members and consulting firms. The program was implemented in 2025 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

The QuakeCoRE 2025 Annual Meeting (QCAM25) is a flagship event organized by Te Hiranga Rū QuakeCoRE, Aotearoa New Zealand’s Centre of Research Excellence, focused on transforming earthquake resilience through integrated research and collaboration. Held in Dunedin, the meeting brought together researchers, practitioners, policymakers, and international partners to accelerate the translation of earthquake science into societal impact. The event featured plenaries, poster sessions, workshops, and networking activities, emphasizing a system-level approach to earthquake resilience.

Key Highlights

Record participation with 270 attendees and 111 posters, indicating community growth and research productivity., 
International representation from six countries, enhancing global knowledge exchange., 
Distinguished Lecture by Helen Crowley on seismic risk modeling, emphasizing quantitative decision-support., 
Introduction of New Zealand’s first National Liquefaction Model, marking a milestone in hazard quantification., 
Structured engagement with Student Chapters to foster next-generation leadership in earthquake resilience.

Results

270 participants, the largest cohort to date, indicating expanding engagement., 
111 posters, the largest collection to date, reflecting a diverse and active research portfolio., 
International participation from six countries, broadening comparative perspectives and collaborations.
