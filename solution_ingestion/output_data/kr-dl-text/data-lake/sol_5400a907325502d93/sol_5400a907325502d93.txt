Introduction

This solution, Project AF8: Alpine Fault Magnitude 8, 
South Island Alpine Fault Earthquake Response (SAFER) Framework addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in New Zealand between Six South Island CDEM Groups, Emergency Management Southland, Emergency Management Marlborough, University of Canterbury, University of Otago, QuakeCoRE, and Resilience to Nature’s Challenges (RNC-Rural). The program was implemented in 2016 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

Project AF8 is a comprehensive initiative aimed at preparing for a potential magnitude 8 earthquake along New Zealand's Alpine Fault. It involves a collaborative effort among multiple agencies, integrating scientific research with emergency management practices to enhance resilience. The project focuses on developing actionable risk knowledge and impact modeling to support emergency management and lifeline planning. Key outputs include the AF8 Hazard Scenario and the SAFER Framework, which guide coordinated response planning across the South Island.

Key Highlights

The SAFER Framework provides a formalized week-one response blueprint for post-earthquake operations, emphasizing mutual aid and sustainable actions. The project employs a science-practice co-creation model, aligning with the UN Sendai Framework for disaster risk governance. It integrates scenario intensity models with infrastructure networks to identify vulnerabilities and prioritize response actions. The initiative is led by all six South Island CDEM Groups, ensuring regional consistency and shared standards.

Results

The AF8 Hazard Scenario and SAFER Framework have been widely adopted by organizations for emergency management and business continuity planning. The project has contributed to the richest Alpine Fault paleoearthquake record, enhancing forecasting reliability and maintaining active engagement to drive preparedness.
