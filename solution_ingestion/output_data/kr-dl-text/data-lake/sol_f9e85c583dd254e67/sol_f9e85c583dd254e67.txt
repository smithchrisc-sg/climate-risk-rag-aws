Introduction

This solution, Waipaoa Flood Control Climate Change Resilience Project (WFCS Upgrade) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in New Zealand between Gisborne District Council, Government of New Zealand, KiwiRail, and Earthwork Solutions Ltd. The program was implemented in 2019 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The Waipaoa Flood Control Climate Change Resilience Project is an extensive upgrade of the Waipaoa Flood Control Scheme in Tairāwhiti/Gisborne, New Zealand. Managed by the Gisborne District Council, the project aims to enhance flood protection to withstand a 1-in-100-year flood event, considering climate change impacts up to 2090. The project involves strengthening 64 km of stopbanks, widening crests, and raising heights, along with additional flood control measures. It is co-funded by the central government and aims to protect 10,000 hectares of productive floodplain and the city of Gisborne.

Key Highlights

The project is a flagship element of Gisborne District Council's flood resilience portfolio, accelerating completion by four years to 2027. It includes significant engineering upgrades such as widening and raising stopbanks, installing floodgates, and rock armouring. The project is strategically aligned with national climate resilience goals and leverages government co-funding to expedite delivery.

Results

As of the latest reporting, approximately 32 km of stopbanks have been upgraded. The project has secured $20.46 million in external funding and $10.1 million from the Gisborne District Council's 2024–2027 Three-Year Plan.
