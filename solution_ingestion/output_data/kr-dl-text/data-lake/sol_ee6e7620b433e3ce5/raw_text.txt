Introduction

This solution, Disaster Recovery Funding Arrangements (DRFA) addresses natural catastrophe in Australia between Commonwealth of Australia, Emergency Management Australia (EMA), National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA), Services Australia, Department of Social Services, State and Territory Governments, and Local Governments. The program was implemented in 2018 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The Disaster Recovery Funding Arrangements (DRFA) is Australia's primary national framework for sharing the costs of disaster relief and recovery between the Commonwealth and state/territory governments. Established in 2018, it replaced previous arrangements and provides structured assistance categories (A-D), advance payments for severe events, and encourages reinvestment of reconstruction efficiencies into natural hazard mitigation. The DRFA operates under the Intergovernmental Agreement on Federal Financial Relations and is supported by other policy instruments like the Social Security Act 1991, tax concessions, and the Emergency Response Fund. It involves multiple public bodies, including Emergency Management Australia and the National Emergency Management Agency, with Services Australia delivering financial relief to affected individuals.

Key Highlights

Four-tiered assistance model (Categories A-D) for targeted support, 
Up to 75% reimbursement of eligible state expenditures, 
Advance payments mechanism for severe disasters, 
Mitigation reinvestment to strengthen resilience, 
Complementarity with Social Security Act payments and the Emergency Response Fund

Results

Principal mechanism for Commonwealth financial assistance in disaster recovery, 
Up to 75% reimbursement reduces fiscal pressure on subnational governments
