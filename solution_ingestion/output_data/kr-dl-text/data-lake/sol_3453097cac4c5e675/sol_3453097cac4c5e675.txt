Introduction

This solution, National Volcanic Hazard and Risk Model (NVHRM) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in New Zealand between Toka Tū Ake Natural Hazards Commission, National Emergency Management Agency/MCDEM, DPMC, Treasury, MFAT, LINZ, MBIE, local government and CDEM groups, MetService, DOC, NZ Lifelines Council, iwi and Iwi Leaders Forum, University of Auckland, University of Canterbury, and World Bank GFDRR. The program was implemented in 2022 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

The National Volcanic Hazard and Risk Model (NVHRM) is a comprehensive framework designed to quantify volcanic hazards, exposure, vulnerability, and risk in New Zealand. It integrates scientific research with stakeholder needs to inform planning, policy, and risk transfer decisions. The model is part of the Hazard and Risk Management programme led by GNS Science, now within Earth Sciences New Zealand, and is funded by New Zealand's Strategic Science Investment Fund. NVHRM aims to provide a consistent national standard for assessing volcanic risks, supporting emergency management, infrastructure planning, and insurance strategies.

Key Highlights

NVHRM employs a co-design approach, integrating both scientific rigor and stakeholder needs, which is uncommon in national risk models., 
The model is aligned with New Zealand's risk transfer ecosystem, ensuring outputs are actionable for financial resilience and recovery planning., 
It supports long-term infrastructure planning, considering climate change, urbanization, and coastal densification., 
NVHRM specifically addresses the Auckland Volcanic Field, providing practical outputs for metropolitan emergency management and land-use planning., 
The model accommodates qualitative, semi-quantitative, and quantitative methods, allowing for usable outputs even with incomplete data.

Results

NVHRM has progressed from scoping to structured co-development with research and practitioner communities., 
It is integrated into HRM's national mandate, collaborating with a wide range of agencies to establish consistent national risk standards., 
The model supports the public insurer Toka Tū Ake in quantifying potential losses from future volcanic events, aiding in underwriting and risk management strategies.
