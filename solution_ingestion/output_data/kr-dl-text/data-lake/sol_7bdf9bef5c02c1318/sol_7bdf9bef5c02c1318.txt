Introduction

This solution, State highway and local roads recovery funding (Budget 2024) addresses natural catastrophe in New Zealand between New Zealand Government, NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi, Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet (DPMC), Affected local councils, Regional Transport Organizations, and Local Government New Zealand (LGNZ). The program was implemented in 2024 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The State highway and local roads recovery funding initiative, as part of New Zealand's Budget 2024, aims to address the damage caused by severe weather events in 2023, such as Cyclone Gabrielle and the Auckland Anniversary floods. The initiative focuses on both reactive recovery funding to repair damaged roads and proactive resilience investments to mitigate future risks. It involves multiple public organizations, including the New Zealand Government, NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi, and local councils, to ensure a coordinated response to weather-related disruptions.

Key Highlights

The initiative utilizes a National Resilience Tool to prioritize risks from 'low to critical,' ensuring efficient allocation of resources. It streamlines local road funding through Regional Land Transport Plans (RLTPs) to reduce the burden on councils. The program emphasizes a balanced approach, incorporating both green and grey infrastructure, and focuses on access-critical low-volume roads and freight lifelines.

Results

Budget 2024 allocated nearly $1 billion for recovery and resilience projects in regions affected by the 2023 weather events. An additional NZ$219 million was allocated in Budget 2025 for local road recovery in five North Island councils. The Crown Resilience Programme (CRP) set aside NZ$419 million over seven years for proactive resilience measures, with NZ$279 million for state highways and NZ$140 million for local roads.
