Introduction

This solution, CREWS–Papua New Guinea: Weather and Climate Early Warning System (CREWS–PNG) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Papua New Guinea between Papua New Guinea National Weather Service (PNG NWS), Departments of Transport and Infrastructure, Department of Environment and Conservation, Department of Agriculture and Livestock, Department of Forestry, Department of Commerce and Industry, World Meteorological Organization (WMO), Australian Bureau of Meteorology (BoM), National Institute of Water and Atmospheric Research (NIWA), and New Zealand. The program was implemented in 2017 and focuses on collaboration, leveraging tech, and raising awareness.

Description

CREWS–PNG is an initiative under the global Climate Risk and Early Warning Systems (CREWS) international initiative, aimed at strengthening hydrometeorological services and impact-based early warning systems in Papua New Guinea. It focuses on building an end-to-end early warning system for drought, improving observational networks, and enhancing forecasting and service delivery for climate-sensitive sectors.

Key Highlights

The initiative features an end-to-end early warning system design focused on drought, integrating upstream observations, forecasting, and downstream advisory delivery. It extends forecast lead times from 24 hours to up to 7 days, significantly improving preparedness windows. The project also emphasizes subseasonal-to-seasonal prediction capacity, crucial for drought monitoring and anticipatory action. Strong regional technical partnerships with organizations like the Australian Bureau of Meteorology facilitate the transfer of methods and operational know-how tailored to PNG’s needs.

Results

The project has strengthened the institutional capacity of PNG NWS, expanded service delivery, and integrated operational drought monitoring and early warning systems. It has extended forecasting horizons to up to 7 days, addressing stakeholder demand for more actionable windows, and established improved drought monitoring and early warning systems supporting strategic decision-making in various sectors.
