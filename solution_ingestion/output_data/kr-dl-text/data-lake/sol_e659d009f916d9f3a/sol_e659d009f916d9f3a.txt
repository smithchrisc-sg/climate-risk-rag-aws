Introduction

This solution, Upgrade of the National Disaster Centre’s Information Management System (IMS) addresses natural catastrophe in Papua New Guinea between National Disaster Centre (NDC), National Weather Service (NWS), Port Moresby Geophysical Observatory (PMGO), Department of Community Development and Religion, Department of National Education, Department of Information, Communication and Technology (DICT), Pacific Community (SPC), Geoscience, Energy and Maritime (GEM) Division, European Union (EU), Organisation of African, and Caribbean and Pacific States (OACPS). The program was implemented in 2025 and focuses on collaboration, leveraging tech, and raising awareness.

Description

The upgrade of the National Disaster Centre’s Information Management System (IMS) in Papua New Guinea is a key component of the EU-funded Building Safety and Resilience in the Pacific Phase II (BSRP II) programme. This initiative aims to enhance the country's disaster management capabilities by integrating advanced data collection, hazard monitoring, and communication systems. The IMS will serve as the central hub for aggregating and analyzing hazard data, facilitating real-time decision-making, and improving communication with remote communities and at-risk groups.

Key Highlights

Integration with a new National Multi-Hazard Early Warning Centre to provide reliable upstream feeds for faster warnings., 
Broadband seismic upgrade at Lae to improve geohazard characterization., 
First integrated database of vulnerable populations to enable targeted alerting and assistance., 
Targeted digital communication systems for last-mile delivery to remote communities., 
Government-wide capacity-building with KoBo/GIS training for 21 officials.

Results

21 government officials trained in data collection and GIS, including 11 women and one participant with visual impairment., 
Establishment of a National Steering Committee for governance and coordination., 
Part of a EUR 14 million investment across 14 Pacific Island countries and Timor-Leste., 
Expected outcomes include faster warning cycles, improved situational awareness, and better inclusion in preparedness and response.
