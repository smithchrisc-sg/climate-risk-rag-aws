The program was implemented in 2024 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

The Cyclone Recovery Capability Fund (CRCF) is a targeted initiative under New Zealand's Budget 2024, designed to enhance the capability of local councils to manage recovery functions following major weather disasters, such as Cyclone Gabrielle. The fund focuses on providing technical expertise and capacity to accelerate recovery processes, including voluntary buyouts for high-risk properties, flood risk assessments, and infrastructure restoration. It aims to address bottlenecks in decision-making and project delivery, ensuring faster and more effective recovery efforts.

Key Highlights

The CRCF is unique in its focus on enhancing capability rather than funding capital works. It provides funding for technical investigations, project management, and risk categorization to accelerate recovery processes. The fund supports managed retreat and voluntary buyouts in high-risk areas, particularly in Auckland and Gisborne. It aligns with broader climate resilience and recovery initiatives, complementing over $1 billion in cyclone-related funding.

Results

The CRCF allocates approximately $16.8 million across 12 councils for 50 projects. Notable allocations include $4.89 million for Auckland Council to accelerate land categorization and voluntary buyouts, and $4.75 million for Gisborne District Council for multiple recovery initiatives. The fund aims to shorten decision and delivery timelines, enabling faster recovery and resilience building.
