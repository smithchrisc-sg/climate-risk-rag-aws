Introduction

This solution, World Bank Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Program (DRFI/DRFIP); Pacific Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Program (Pacific DRFIP) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Papua New Guinea between World Bank Group, Global Facility for Disaster Reduction and Recovery (GFDRR), Secretariat of the Pacific Community (SPC/SOPAC), Government of Japan, ACP–EU Natural Disaster Risk Reduction Program, Risk modelers, Insurers, Reinsurers, and Investment banks. The program was implemented in 2013 and focuses on collaboration and leveraging tech.

Description

The World Bank's Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Program (DRFI/DRFIP) aims to help governments manage the fiscal impacts of natural disasters. In the Pacific, this is executed through the Pacific Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Program (Pacific DRFIP), which pilots sovereign parametric catastrophe insurance and strengthens public financial management for disasters across Pacific Island Countries, including Papua New Guinea. The program provides technical assistance and financial protection instruments to enhance fiscal resilience and reduce reliance on ad hoc post-disaster financing.

Key Highlights

The program features parametric triggers for rapid payouts, regional risk analytics via PCRAFI, and strong public-private partnerships with insurers and reinsurers. It integrates with public financial management to improve budget reliability and reduce emergency borrowing. The program is backed by donors like the Government of Japan and involves extensive collaboration with private sector partners for risk modeling and insurance.

Results

Since 1950, extreme natural events in the Pacific have affected over 9.2 million people and caused approximately US$3.2 billion in damage. The pilot program initially included countries like Marshall Islands, Samoa, Solomon Islands, Tonga, and Vanuatu, with Cook Islands joining later. Although Papua New Guinea was not part of the initial insured cohort, the program's framework remains accessible for future adoption.
