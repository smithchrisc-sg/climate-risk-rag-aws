Introduction

This solution, Natural Hazards Commission Toka Tū Ake: Risk Reduction Framework and Tools (2024–2025) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in New Zealand between Natural Hazards Commission Toka Tū Ake, Central and Local Government, NEMA, Regional Councils, World Forum of Catastrophe Programmes, World Bank, BRANZ, MBIE, NZ Geotechnical Society, NZSEE, SESOC, GNS Science, NIWA, and University of Auckland. The program was implemented in 2024 and focuses on collaboration, raising awareness, and regulation.

Description

The Natural Hazards Commission Toka Tū Ake, formerly known as the Earthquake Commission, is a public insurance scheme in New Zealand with a statutory mandate to reduce the impact of natural hazards. The initiative focuses on a risk reduction framework that includes a Risk Reduction Strategy, a Natural Hazards Portal, action plans for land use and resilient buildings, and a risk tolerance methodology. It aims to enable evidence-based decisions by homeowners, councils, and the wider sector, leveraging international leadership and legislative mandates to influence long-term resilience choices.

Key Highlights

The initiative features a legislative mandate for risk reduction, a transparent Natural Hazards Portal providing property-level claims history, and a codified risk tolerance methodology. It integrates with national hazard modelling initiatives and promotes proactive resilience through policy influence and sector engagement. The initiative also emphasizes public education and future workforce development through partnerships with museums and schools.

Results

Documented outputs include enhancements to the Natural Hazards Portal, active strategic frameworks, ongoing policy submissions, and sector capability-building. While public quantification of outcomes is limited, the initiative has shown clear deliverables and uptake by decision-makers using related hazard models and datasets.
