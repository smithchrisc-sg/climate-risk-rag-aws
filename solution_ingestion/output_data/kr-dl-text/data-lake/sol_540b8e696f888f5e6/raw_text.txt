Introduction

This solution, Coastal Protection Concepts for Greater Southern Waterfront (GSW) and Changi — City–East Coast Site‑Specific Study (SSS) addresses natural catastrophe in Singapore between PUB, Singapore’s National Water Agency, Urban Redevelopment Authority (URA), Singapore Land Authority (SLA), Land Transport Authority (LTA), and National Climate Change Secretariat (NCCS). The program was implemented in 2021 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The Coastal Protection Concepts for Greater Southern Waterfront (GSW) and Changi is a strategic initiative led by Singapore's PUB to address sea-level rise and coastal flooding. It is part of a broader national program for coastal protection and climate adaptation, integrated with the Urban Redevelopment Authority’s Greater Southern Waterfront urban transformation. The initiative involves constructing coastal barriers, tidal gates, and raised structures to form a continuous line of defense for the City–East Coast region.

Key Highlights

The initiative features default-open gated coastal barriers to preserve marine exchange and navigational access, closing only during extreme tide events. It integrates with Singapore's major urban regeneration project, ensuring flood resilience is a placemaking asset. The approach includes modular tailoring of solutions for different shoreline segments and is backed by national elevation standards and existing hard-protection coverage.

Results

The City–East Coast Site-Specific Study (SSS) was completed in 2025 with detailed recommendations, costing S$18 million. The initiative is set to begin construction in the 2030s, aligning with urban regeneration timelines. Quantifiable hazard-reduction outcomes are not yet available due to the recent concept release.
