Introduction

This solution, Transboundary Disaster Risk Assessment and Scenario Planning for Tropical Cyclone and Droughts in the ASEAN Region addresses natural catastrophe in Cambodia, 
Philippines, 
Viet Nam between ASEAN Secretariat, ASEAN Socio-Cultural Community (ASCC) Knowledge Hub, and Asian Development Bank (ADB). The program was implemented in 2024 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

The initiative, officially named 'Transboundary Disaster Risk Assessment and Scenario Planning for Tropical Cyclone and Droughts in the ASEAN Region,' focuses on modeling transboundary risk scenarios for Cambodia, the Philippines, and Viet Nam. It addresses the impacts of tropical cyclones and droughts, amplified by climate change, and aims to provide forward-looking planning insights for ASEAN Member States (AMS) policy processes. The study emphasizes the inclusion of sex, age, and disability disaggregated data to ensure equity-aware resilience strategies.

Key Highlights

The initiative uniquely applies a transboundary lens to assess risks that do not respect borders, such as tropical cyclones and droughts. It quantifies future housing damages and drought probabilities, emphasizing the need for joint assessment and scenario planning. The study highlights uneven impacts and cross-border risk corridors, such as the Mekong, and provides policy-relevant scenario planning for priority AMS. It also identifies localized hot spots, such as northern and central Viet Nam's coastal provinces and northern/central Cambodia.

Results

The study projects that tropical cyclones could damage up to 182,000 residential houses by 2050 across the three focus countries, a 60% increase from the current 114,000 annually. Drought probability is projected to rise 60–80% among the three countries, with Cambodia facing a worst-case 46% increase in dry-season drought probability by 2080. The study also identifies transboundary drought spans from Cambodia to southern Viet Nam across the Mekong Delta.
