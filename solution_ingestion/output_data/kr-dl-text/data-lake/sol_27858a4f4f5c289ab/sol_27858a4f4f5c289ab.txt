Description

The Coastal and Estuarine Risk Mitigation Program (CERMP) is an initiative by the Australian Government aimed at reducing disaster risks for coastal and estuarine communities across Australia. Announced in 2022 and operational from March 2023, the program provides $50 million for projects focused on infrastructure, planning, and monitoring. It operates under the Emergency Response Fund framework and is part of the broader Disaster Ready Fund, with the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) overseeing its administration. The program supports state-led administration and local delivery of projects, focusing on adaptation and resilience measures, planning, and monitoring activities.

Key Highlights

CERMP is a national program that integrates both nature-based and engineered solutions to address coastal risks. It emphasizes a mixed portfolio approach, including grey infrastructure like seawalls and green-blue solutions such as dune restoration. The program also invests significantly in monitoring infrastructure, exemplified by Queensland's $4.3 million State-wide Nearshore Bathymetry Survey. This survey uses airborne sensors to collect high-resolution data for improved hazard assessment and modeling. CERMP's governance involves intergovernmental collaboration, with states administering funds and local councils implementing projects.

Results

$50 million total funding allocated. In Queensland, $8.4 million was distributed across 10 projects, including a $4.3 million statewide bathymetry survey. In Tasmania, Sorell Council received $1,868,509 for projects like dune remediation and stormwater upgrades, with additional co-investment from the council. Break O’Day Council also received funding for risk mitigation projects.
