Introduction

This solution, Philippines: Disaster Resilience Improvement Program (DRIP) addresses natural catastrophe in Philippines between Government of the Philippines, Local Government Units (LGUs), Asian Development Bank (ADB), InsuResilience Global Partnership, and Asia-Pacific Climate Finance Fund (ACliFF). The program was implemented in 2020 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The Disaster Resilience Improvement Program (DRIP) is a policy-based operation by the Asian Development Bank aimed at enhancing the Philippines' resilience to natural hazards and public health emergencies, including pandemics. With a loan of US$500 million, DRIP focuses on three reform pillars: improving policy and institutional arrangements for disaster risk management and health emergencies, enhancing the resilience of local government units and national assets, and developing disaster risk financing solutions.

Key Highlights

DRIP provides rapid access to emergency financing while driving medium-term reforms. It uniquely integrates pandemic preparedness into disaster risk management and supports city-level disaster insurance development. The program emphasizes effective gender mainstreaming and nationwide coverage, with safeguards categorized as Category C for Environment, Involuntary Resettlement, and Indigenous Peoples.

Results

The program successfully implemented US$500 million in policy-based financing, with reforms executed across policy/institutional, LGU resilience, and risk financing areas. It complemented broader donor efforts to stabilize macro-financing during COVID-19 shocks.
