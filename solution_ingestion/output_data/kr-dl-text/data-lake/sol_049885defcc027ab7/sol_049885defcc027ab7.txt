Introduction

This solution, Start Network's Disaster Risk Financing (DRF) pilots in Pakistan addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Pakistan between Pakistan's National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), Pakistan Meteorological Department, University of Reading, GlobalAgRisk, Humanitarian Innovation Fund, Asian Development Bank, United Kingdom's Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO), and Government of the Netherlands. The program was implemented in 2017 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

Start Network's Disaster Risk Financing (DRF) pilots in Pakistan are a nationally led, NGO-driven system designed to enable anticipatory and early response to predictable perils such as droughts, floods, and heatwaves. The initiative uses scientific hazard models, pre-agreed contingency plans, and pre-positioned finance to operationalize forecast-based, parametric-style releases of funds linked to clear triggers. It is a continuation and scaling of Start Network's earlier Drought Financing Facility work initiated with Pakistan's NDMA in 2017.

Key Highlights

The initiative is unique in being NGO-led but co-designed with government and technical partners. It prioritizes the secondary (winter) cropping season for drought risk, filling a gap in traditional monitoring programs. The drought component is built in partnership with the University of Reading on the TAMSAT-ALERT platform, which predicts vegetation indices to anticipate agricultural stress. The system emphasizes locally led governance to accelerate decision-making and ensure alignment with national and provincial priorities.

Results

The DRF has operationalized provincial drought triggers with documented forecasting accuracy assessments. It has expanded multi-hazard coverage, with the heatwave model covering 12 districts, the flood model covering five major rivers, and the drought model spanning Punjab, Sindh, and Balochistan. The DRF has been used for anticipatory allocations in 2023–2025 and during 2025 floods. Model outputs have been adopted by non-member agencies, indicating broader institutional uptake.
