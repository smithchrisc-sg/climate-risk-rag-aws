Introduction

This solution, Cambodia Sustainable Landscape and Ecotourism Project (CSLEP) addresses natural catastrophe in Kingdom of Cambodia between Ministry of Environment (MoE), Ministry of Rural Development (MRD), World Bank, and Global Environment Facility (GEF). The program was implemented in 2019 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

The Cambodia Sustainable Landscape and Ecotourism Project (CSLEP) is an initiative aimed at improving the management of protected areas and enhancing ecotourism and non-timber forest product value chains to boost rural livelihoods in the Cardamom Mountains–Tonle Sap landscape. The project, supported by the World Bank and the Global Environment Facility, focuses on landscape-scale conservation, sustainable livelihoods, and infrastructure connectivity across a 3.8 million hectare area, aligning with Cambodia's goals for protected area management and nature-based economic development.

Key Highlights

The project covers seven protected areas across seven provinces, emphasizing ecotourism as a driver for conservation and livelihoods. It includes a comprehensive safeguards suite to manage social risks, such as resettlement and Indigenous Peoples' rights. The project also focuses on improving access and connectivity to make ecotourism sites and markets more reachable, with significant investments in infrastructure like roads and bridges.

Results

The project has operational components with a focus on ecotourism services. Infrastructure improvements and value-chain actions, such as access bridges and bamboo processing initiatives, have been reported. The overall implementation is rated 'Moderately Satisfactory' as of August 2025.
