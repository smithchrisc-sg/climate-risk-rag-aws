Introduction

This solution, Sindh Resilience Project (SRP) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Pakistan between Islamic Republic of Pakistan, Sindh Irrigation Department (SID), Provincial Disaster Management Authority (PDMA), Government of Sindh, International Development Association (IDA), World Bank Group, and Associated Consulting Engineers (ACE) (Pvt) Ltd. The program was implemented in 2016 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The Sindh Resilience Project (SRP) is a government initiative in Pakistan, financed by the International Development Association of the World Bank, aimed at mitigating flood and drought risks in Sindh Province. It combines structural investments, such as strengthening Indus River flood embankments and constructing small dams, with institutional capacity building for disaster risk management. The project also includes a Contingent Emergency Response Component for rapid financing reallocation during crises.

Key Highlights

The SRP features a dual-hazard design focusing on both flood and drought risks, a unique approach for provincial projects. It includes a Contingent Emergency Response Component that allowed rapid fund reallocation to address the COVID-19 emergency. The project also emphasizes strong environmental and social safeguards and utilizes digital tools for data-driven irrigation operations. Additional Financing proposed by the World Bank in 2020 expanded the project's scope to include public health emergencies.

Results

By mid-2020, the SRP completed over 200 km of reinforced flood defenses and 6 small dams, with all 15 dams contracted. These efforts enhance water security and reduce flood risks. Additionally, up to US$10 million was reallocated to support the COVID-19 response, demonstrating the project's flexibility and institutional agility.
