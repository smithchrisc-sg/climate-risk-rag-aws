Introduction

This solution, Nepal’s Government-led Earthquake Risk Reduction and Mitigation Initiatives (2020–2025) addresses natural catastrophe in Nepal between National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA), Ministry of Home Affairs (MoHA), Ministry of Federal Affairs and General Administration (MoFAGA), National Council for Disaster Risk Reduction and Management (NCDRRM), World Bank, USAID/Nepal, UN Country Team, ESCAP, and UN agencies designated as Provincial Focal Point Agencies (PFPAs). The program was implemented in 2020 and focuses on collaboration, raising awareness, and accessibility.

Description

Nepal’s Government-led Earthquake Risk Reduction and Mitigation Initiatives (2020–2025) is a comprehensive program aimed at reducing earthquake risks through a multi-tiered governance approach. It is anchored in the Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act, 2074 (2017) and executed by the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA). The initiative involves federal policy reforms, decentralized implementation across 753 municipalities, evidence-led risk mapping, large-scale capacity building, digital learning, inclusive planning, and community mapping. It is supported by policy-linked risk financing from the World Bank and technical partners.

Key Highlights

The initiative features deep decentralization to 753 municipalities, integration of scientific and indigenous knowledge, inclusive training and e-learning, and community-led mapping at a national scale. It also includes risk-informed policy reforms backed by the Catastrophe Deferred Drawdown Option (Cat DDO) and routine high-level governance via the National Council for Disaster Risk Reduction and Management (NCDRRM).

Results

The initiative trained over 19,900 local officials, advanced local legal frameworks and disaster management committees, increased municipal DRR budgets, mapped over 130,000 buildings, and involved 6,000 volunteers in mapping 80% of affected areas post-2015 quake. It also facilitated operational coordination during COVID-19 and the 2023 Jajarkot earthquake, and approved reconstruction for the 2079 Doti earthquake.
