Introduction

This solution, Regional Infrastructure Fund (RIF) – Flood Resilience Co‑Funding (Budget 2024) addresses natural catastrophe in New Zealand between Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), New Zealand Treasury, and Te Uru Kahika. The program was implemented in 2024 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The Regional Infrastructure Fund (RIF) – Flood Resilience Co‑Funding is a New Zealand government initiative launched in July 2024 as part of Budget 2024. It aims to enhance regional flood resilience through a dedicated $200 million fund within the broader $1.2 billion RIF. The fund supports regional economic growth and infrastructure improvement by providing capital and operational expenditure for flood resilience projects. The initiative involves co-funding from regional and unitary councils, with a focus on mitigating flood risks exacerbated by climate change.

Key Highlights

The RIF features a ring-fenced $200 million allocation specifically for flood resilience, emphasizing targeted risk reduction. It combines capital expenditure for long-term infrastructure investments with operational expenditure for grants and fund operations. The initiative promotes a 60/40 cost-sharing model between central government and local councils, ensuring local accountability and alignment with regional plans. Monthly public reporting ensures transparency and accountability, while the program's economic rationale is supported by evidence that each $1 invested in flood resilience saves approximately $8 in post-event costs.

Results

As of mid-2025, the RIF has committed $771.2 million, with $397.3 million approved and $30.1 million disbursed. The flood resilience stream has earmarked $101 million for 42 projects under the 'Before the Deluge' portfolio, with councils contributing around 40% of project costs. The initiative is in its early execution phase, focusing on consolidating and delivering flood resilience projects.
