CSIRO Drought Resilience Mission

Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (CSIRO), 
Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry (DAFF), 
Bureau of Meteorology (BoM)

Introduction

This solution, CSIRO Drought Resilience Mission addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Australia between Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (CSIRO), Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry (DAFF), and Bureau of Meteorology (BoM). The program was implemented in 2021 and focuses on collaboration, leveraging tech, and raising awareness.

Description

The CSIRO Drought Resilience Mission is an initiative aimed at reducing the impacts of drought in Australia by 30% by 2030. It is part of CSIRO's larger Missions program, which addresses national challenges through collaborative R&D efforts. The mission focuses on climate-related drought risks affecting agriculture, water security, and regional communities. It employs a three-level intervention model that includes on-farm innovation, regional resilience, and policy enablers to tackle drought as a systemic risk.

Key Highlights

The mission sets a clear, measurable target of reducing drought impacts by 30% by 2030. It employs a systems approach integrating on-farm technology, regional water security, and policy/data enablers. Key components include the Climate Services for Agriculture program, a national drought map, soil loss and wind erosion risk tools, and water banking technologies. The mission also emphasizes Indigenous knowledge integration and has a governance model that fosters scalability through 'crowd-in' co-investment mechanisms.

Results

Development of a national drought map for Australia, 
Initiation of the Climate Services for Agriculture program, 
Completion of a water banking opportunity assessment in the Murray–Darling Basin, 
Tools created for identifying soil loss risks due to wind erosion
