Introduction

This solution, Earthquake-Related Government Risk Financing Initiatives in Myanmar (2020–2025) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Myanmar between National Disaster Management Committee (NDMC), Ministry of Planning, Finance and Industry (MOPFI), Ministry of Planning and Finance (MoPF), Relief and Resettlement Department (RRD), Department of Meteorology and Hydrology (DMH), World Bank, Global Facility for Disaster Reduction and Recovery (GFDRR), Southeast Asia Disaster Risk Insurance Facility (SEADRIF), ASEAN Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance Programme (ADRFI), AHA Centre, United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction (UNDRR), and Regional reinsurers and potential SEADRIF Insurance Company counterparties. The program was implemented in 2020 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The Earthquake-Related Government Risk Financing Initiatives in Myanmar (2020–2025) encompass a series of policy, budgetary, and regional engagement measures aimed at protecting public finances against seismic shocks. The initiative is built on a legal and institutional framework that includes a Disaster Management Fund, ex-ante budgetary instruments, contingent financing in development projects, and participation in ASEAN regional programs for disaster risk financing and insurance. Despite the political crisis in 2021, Myanmar made modest institutional progress in disaster risk financing.

Key Highlights

The initiative is characterized by its multi-layered approach, including a legal and institutional foundation with the Natural Disaster Management Law and Disaster Management Fund, ex-ante budgetary instruments like contingency appropriations, and participation in regional programs such as SEADRIF and ADRFI. It also involves contingent financing through World Bank projects with a Contingent Emergency Response Component (CERC).

Results

The initiative faced constraints due to the domestic political crisis from 2021, limiting new sovereign risk transfer operations and visible earthquake-specific financial results. The risk financing posture remained dominated by budgetary reserves and ad hoc reallocations rather than pre-arranged, rules-based instruments.
