Introduction

This solution, PNG Tsunami Guide and Tsunami Hazard Capability Building addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Papua New Guinea between Port Moresby Geophysical Observatory, National Disaster Centre (NDC), Department of Works and Highways (DoWH), Climate Change and Development Authority (CCDA), UNESCO Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission (IOC), Pacific Tsunami Warning Center (PTWC), IOC-UNESCO Tsunami Programme, Global Facility for Disaster Reduction and Recovery (GFDRR), ThinkHazard, Global Green Growth Institute (GGGI), and GHD. The program was implemented in Ongoing; institutional foundations predate 2011 and focuses on collaboration, raising awareness, and regulation.

Description

The PNG Tsunami Guide initiative is a comprehensive program aimed at enhancing tsunami risk management in Papua New Guinea. It focuses on developing practical guidance and strengthening capabilities from hazard assessment and warning to land-use planning, vertical evacuation, and resilient infrastructure planning. The initiative is part of the broader IOC-UNESCO Tsunami Programme and PNG's disaster risk management and climate-resilient infrastructure initiatives.

Key Highlights

The initiative addresses a high national tsunami hazard with a ≥40% chance of a damaging tsunami in 50 years. It features an end-to-end warning chain anchored in the PTWC and national focal points, emphasizes vertical evacuation, and integrates return-period design thinking and community consultation on coastal defenses. It is also linked with broader resilience and infrastructure capacity-building efforts through the IRIS PNG program.

Results

Specific quantifiable outcomes for the PNG Tsunami Guide are not published. However, PNG maintains designated national tsunami contacts and focal points, is integrated into PTWS/ITIC, and has ongoing capacity strengthening via IRIS PNG. The initiative targets a coastal population at risk of over 600,900 people, approximately 5.6% of PNG's population.
