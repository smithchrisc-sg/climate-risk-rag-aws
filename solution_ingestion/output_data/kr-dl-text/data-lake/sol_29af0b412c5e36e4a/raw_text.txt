Introduction

This solution, Rangitata River 2019 Flood Recovery addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in New Zealand between Environment Canterbury (ECan), Central government, Waka Kotahi NZ Transport Agency, KiwiRail, Transpower, Regional/district councils, Department of Conservation (DOC), Rangitata South Irrigation Ltd, Te Kete Tipuranga o Huirapa, and Local contractors. The program was implemented in 2021 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

The Rangitata River 2019 Flood Recovery initiative was launched in response to a historic flood event in December 2019 in Canterbury, New Zealand. The program, led by Environment Canterbury, aims to restore, strengthen, and adapt flood protection systems along the Rangitata River. It combines engineered structures with natural infrastructure and scientific guidance to enhance resilience against future flooding events. The initiative is part of a broader regional flood-resilience program and involves multiple public and private partners.

Key Highlights

The initiative employs a 'grey-green' approach, integrating hard engineering solutions with natural infrastructure. Key measures include the construction of stopbanks, gravel groynes, and rock stub groynes to reinforce flood protection. The program also emphasizes ecological restoration and weed management, with a focus on enhancing resilience beyond pre-flood levels. The project has been a significant regional economic stimulus, engaging over 24 local businesses during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Results

The initiative has completed major recovery works by December 2023, with ongoing resilience planning ('work #2') underway. The project has seen an investment of approximately $8.5 million, with over 60% co-funding from the central government.
