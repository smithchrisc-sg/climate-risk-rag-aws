Introduction

This solution, Drought‑focused Government Risk Financing in New Zealand (2020–2025) addresses natural catastrophe in New Zealand between Financial Markets Authority (FMA), New Zealand Government, Oxfam Aotearoa, and World Health Organization (WHO). The program was implemented in 2020 and focuses on regulation and collaboration.

Description

The Drought‑focused Government Risk Financing initiative in New Zealand (2020–2025) is embedded within a broader climate risk governance framework. It focuses on enhancing climate risk transparency through the climate‑related disclosures regime overseen by the Financial Markets Authority (FMA). This approach aims to improve the financial system's ability to identify, price, and finance climate hazards, including drought, while aligning with international climate finance commitments and public health early warning systems.

Key Highlights

The initiative integrates financial regulation with climate hazard preparedness, emphasizing transparency and governance over debt-funded crisis response. It aligns national policy with international climate finance responsibilities, enhancing the potential to mobilize private capital for resilience. The FMA's climate-related disclosures regime serves as a foundational market enabler for risk financing, improving data on drought exposure and vulnerability.

Results

Quantifiable, program-specific drought-finance outcomes are not documented. However, the publication of FMA’s 2025 supervisory insights indicates that climate risk disclosure practices relevant to drought are being reviewed and improved across reporting entities.
