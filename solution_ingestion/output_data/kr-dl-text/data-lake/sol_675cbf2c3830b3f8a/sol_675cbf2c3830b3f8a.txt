National Resilience Plan 2025–2026

Ministry of Climate Change and Environmental Coordination (MoCC&EC), 
National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)

Introduction

This solution, National Resilience Plan 2025–2026 addresses natural catastrophe in Pakistan between Ministry of Climate Change and Environmental Coordination (MoCC&EC) and National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA). The program was implemented in 2025 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

The National Resilience Plan 2025–2026 is an initiative by Pakistan's federal government to enhance disaster preparedness, response, and recovery, particularly for the monsoon season. The plan, developed by the Ministry of Climate Change and Environmental Coordination and the National Disaster Management Authority, aims to address the fragmentation in the country's disaster risk management system. It focuses on risk mapping, localized hazard-specific readiness, and a unified response framework to mitigate the impacts of floods, cloudbursts, glacial melt, glacial lake outburst floods, landslides, and related monsoon-linked hazards.

Key Highlights

The Plan is outcome-focused, aiming for measurable loss reduction in human life, infrastructure, crops, and livestock. It seeks to integrate fragmented disaster response systems into a single, streamlined framework, improving speed, coordination, and effectiveness. The Plan emphasizes localized hazard-specific preparedness, acknowledging the diverse hazard landscape of Pakistan. It also stresses the importance of early warning systems and public communication to ensure timely protective actions. The Plan is structured to deliver capability enhancements ahead of the 2026 monsoon season.

Results

As of September 26, 2025, the Plan is in its initiation phase with no official quantitative outcomes published. However, it aims to address the baseline conditions of the 2025 monsoon, which resulted in approximately 1,006 deaths and 1,063 injuries, and the 2022 floods, which caused nearly 1,700 deaths and over $30 billion in damage.
