Introduction

This solution, Hōretireti Whenua – Sliding Lands programme addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Aotearoa New Zealand between GNS Science (now part of Earth Sciences New Zealand), Central and local government agencies across New Zealand, Durham University (UK), ETH Zürich (Switzerland), Massey University, Manaaki Whenua Landcare Research, Market Economics, University of Auckland, Resilient Organisations, and University of Canterbury. The program was implemented in 2023 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

The Hōretireti Whenua – Sliding Lands programme is a five-year initiative funded by the MBIE Endeavour Fund, aimed at developing national-scale models to predict and manage landslide hazards in New Zealand. The programme integrates scientific research, socio-economic analysis, and mātauranga Māori to create decision-relevant tools for forecasting landslides triggered by earthquakes and rainfall. It involves a consortium of research institutions, iwi, government, and industry partners, with case studies in Tairāwhiti and Auckland to test its effectiveness.

Key Highlights

The programme is notable for its integration of social science and mātauranga Māori, ensuring community relevance and benefit. It employs probabilistic and scenario-based models to forecast landslides, linking triggering scenarios to susceptibility and impact analysis via RiskScape, and quantifying socio-economic consequences using the MERIT tool. The initiative is supported by a multi-institutional consortium and international expertise, and is recognized within the national professional community.

Results

Early outputs include programme design, initial social-science mapping of decision contexts, case-study frameworks, and doctoral research on dynamic landslide hazard and risk. Full national models and decision-ready products are expected by 2028.
