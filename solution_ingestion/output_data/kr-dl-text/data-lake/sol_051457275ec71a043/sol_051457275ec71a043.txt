Introduction

This solution, USAID’s Tayar Nepal – Improved Disaster Risk Management Project (Tayar Nepal) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Nepal between National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority/Agency (NDRRMA), Municipal Governments, United States Agency for International Development (USAID/Nepal), and DAI Global LLC. The program was implemented in 2019 and focuses on collaboration, raising awareness, and accessibility.

Description

USAID’s Tayar Nepal is a flagship disaster preparedness and risk reduction initiative in Nepal, implemented by DAI Global LLC. The project aims to strengthen national disaster governance, modernize emergency management capabilities, and institutionalize risk-informed municipal planning and community resilience measures across multiple hazards including earthquakes, floods, landslides, fires, and disease outbreaks. It operates in partnership with the Government of Nepal and local governments, with a focus on enhancing disaster-resilient development.

Key Highlights

The project features a national framework for risk-sensitive land use planning (RSLUP), establishment of a National Disaster Training Academy, digitized bundled insurance, and matched municipal/private investments. It emphasizes gender and social inclusion, targeting 50% participation of women and excluded groups in capacity building. The project also supports community-based DRR construction, humanitarian open spaces, and nature-based solutions like planting native saplings for slope stabilization.

Results

The project has benefited over 716,000 people, with more than 35 community DRR construction initiatives protecting over 200,000 individuals. It has produced 4,462 IEC materials on earthquake-resistant construction, planted 3,500 native saplings, and provided digitized bundled insurance to over 1,000 households. Eight municipalities have been strengthened, with targets to have over 10 entities adopting DRM policies and training more than 1,000 DRM personnel.
