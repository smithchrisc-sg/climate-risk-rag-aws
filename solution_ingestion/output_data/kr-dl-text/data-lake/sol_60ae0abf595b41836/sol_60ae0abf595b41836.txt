Introduction

This solution, Coastal Barriers Feasibility Study (CBFS) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Singapore between PUB, Singapore’s National Water Agency, Sentosa Development Corporation (SDC), Coastal Protection and Flood Resilience Institute (CFI) Singapore, and Surbana Jurong. The program was implemented in 2023 and focuses on collaboration and leveraging tech.

Description

The Coastal Barriers Feasibility Study (CBFS) in Singapore is a strategic initiative aimed at addressing long-term sea-level rise and episodic storm surges through the deployment of moveable storm-surge barriers. These barriers are designed to remain open under normal conditions to allow navigation and tidal exchange, and close during extreme events to prevent flooding. The study focuses on the City–East Coast and Greater Southern Waterfront regions, with plans to construct three Dutch-style moveable barriers on the southern coast beginning in the 2030s. The initiative is part of a broader islandwide coastal protection program that divides the national shoreline into eight segments for site-specific planning and delivery.

Key Highlights

First deployment of Dutch-style moveable barriers in Singapore, with three southern barriers planned. The barriers have multi-use potential, serving as transport links and educational/public spaces. The initiative integrates with the Greater Sentosa Master Plan and Long Island reclamation project, aiming for a multifunctional approach to coastal defense. The study involves comprehensive environmental impact assessments to ensure minimal ecological disruption.

Results

PUB has announced the intention to build three southern barriers, marking the completion of the first site-specific coastal protection planning for the City–East Coast. The study covers approximately 116 km of the south-west coast and 16 km around Sentosa, with ongoing environmental and engineering scoping.
