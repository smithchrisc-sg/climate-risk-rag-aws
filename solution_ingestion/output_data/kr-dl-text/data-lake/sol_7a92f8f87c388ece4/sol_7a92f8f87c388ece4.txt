PUB’s Island-wide Drainage Improvement Programme

PUB, Singapore’s National Water Agency, 
Ministry of Sustainability and the Environment (MSE)

Introduction

This solution, PUB’s Island-wide Drainage Improvement Programme addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Singapore between PUB, Singapore’s National Water Agency, and Ministry of Sustainability and the Environment (MSE). The program was implemented in 2011 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

PUB’s Island-wide Drainage Improvement Programme is a comprehensive, multi-year initiative aimed at reducing flood risk in Singapore due to more intense and unpredictable rainfall driven by climate change. The programme involves deepening and widening drains and canals, constructing diversion canals and detention systems, and implementing 'source–pathway–receptor' measures across the urban landscape. It is part of PUB’s holistic stormwater management strategy, overseen by the Ministry of Sustainability and the Environment, and involves stringent design standards, ongoing maintenance, and regulatory requirements for building owners and developers.

Key Highlights

Holistic source–pathway–receptor integration: The programme integrates upstream detention, network conveyance upgrades, and site-level defenses into a coordinated system., 
Large subterranean detention: The Stamford Detention Tank, located beneath the Singapore Botanic Gardens, showcases how major hydraulic storage can be integrated into urban environments., 
Elevated protection thresholds: Strict crest and platform levels for MRT stations and tunnels institutionalize resilience into urban design., 
Lifecycle emphasis: Amendments to the Sewerage and Drainage Act ensure privately installed measures remain functional over time.

Results

Flood-prone areas reduced from approximately 3,200 hectares in the 1970s to less than 25 hectares today., 
Approximately S$2.5 billion invested since 2011 in drainage infrastructure upgrades., 
19 ongoing drainage upgrading works with 6 additional projects to commence in 2025, backed by about S$150 million in FY2025.
