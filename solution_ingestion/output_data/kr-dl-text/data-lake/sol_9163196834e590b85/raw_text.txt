Introduction

This solution, SAP039: Integrated Climate Risk Management for Strengthened Resilience to Climate Change in Buner and Shangla Districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Province, Pakistan addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Pakistan between Government of Pakistan, Government of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, United Nations World Food Programme (WFP), Green Climate Fund (GCF), and Local entrepreneurs and private sector. The program was implemented in 2025 and focuses on collaboration, raising awareness, and accessibility.

Description

The SAP039 project, officially titled 'Integrated Climate Risk Management for Strengthened Resilience to Climate Change in Buner and Shangla Districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Province, Pakistan', is a collaborative initiative led by the United Nations World Food Programme (WFP) and funded by the Green Climate Fund (GCF). It aims to enhance resilience against climate-induced hazards such as flash floods, heavy monsoon rains, and landslides in the vulnerable districts of Buner and Shangla. The project focuses on strengthening early warning systems, providing climate information services, and fostering local adaptation planning to mitigate the impacts of climate variability on food security and livelihoods.

Key Highlights

The project emphasizes early warning-to-early action by integrating hydrometeorological infrastructure with anticipatory protocols and local planning. It includes a gender-transformative design, ensuring that women and persons with disabilities have prioritized access to information and services. The project is embedded within the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa disaster risk management frameworks, enhancing institutional ownership and sustainability. It also engages the private sector and local entrepreneurs to innovate and sustain climate services and adaptation solutions.

Results

The project directly benefits over 1.6 million individuals in Buner and Shangla, with an investment intensity of approximately US$ 6 per beneficiary. It aims to operationalize early warning channels to reduce mortality and livelihood disruption during climate events. The installation of automated weather and hydrometeorological stations is expected to close observational gaps and improve the accuracy of flood and landslide warnings.
