Introduction

This solution, Environment Canterbury Climate Resilience Programme (COVID‑19 shovel‑ready projects) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in New Zealand between Environment Canterbury (ECan), Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), Kānoa – Regional Economic Development & Investment Unit, and Local contractors and specialist firms. The program was implemented in 2020 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

The Environment Canterbury Climate Resilience Programme (CRP) was a portfolio of eight projects implemented between 2020 and 2023 to enhance flood resilience and support regional COVID-19 recovery in the Canterbury region of New Zealand. The programme, co-funded by the central government and local entities, focused on nature-based solutions to manage riverine flood risks, promote biodiversity, and integrate cultural values into flood protection strategies.

Key Highlights

The CRP featured the Regionwide River Berm Transition Programme, which spanned 1,600 km across 23 rivers, focusing on native flora regeneration to improve flood conveyance and biodiversity. The programme integrated soft engineering and wetland enhancement with traditional flood protection, promoting multi-benefit corridors and cultural outcomes. It was supported by a 64% grant from Kānoa and a 36% local share, accelerating intergenerational flood resilience works.

Results

The CRP delivered eight projects on time, with the RRBTP covering 1,600 km of berms. Early outcomes indicated improved berm conditions and positive ecological impacts, though comprehensive quantitative performance data remain limited. The programme advanced mahinga kai opportunities, fish passage access, and recreational upgrades, contributing to long-term ecological resilience.
