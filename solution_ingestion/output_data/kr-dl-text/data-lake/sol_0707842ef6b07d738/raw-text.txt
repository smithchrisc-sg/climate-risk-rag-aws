The National Health Insurance program in Indonesia, known as Jaminan Kesehatan Nasional (JKN), is an universal health coverage (UHC) initiative aimed at providing all Indonesian residents with access to medical services. This program, a vital component of the country's healthcare system, offers a range of services including basic healthcare, advanced medical treatments, and hospitalization. Funded through a combination of wage and employer contributions, JKN is designed to be both inclusive and extensive, covering not only the employee but also their spouse and up to three children, including step-children.

JKN collaborates with private healthcare providers to enhance service delivery and accessibility. A significant milestone in its journey was the approval of a US$400 million loan by the World Bank in 2021, aimed at improving the quality and efficiency of JKN. This effort was further supported by a grant of US$2.33 million from the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation, administered through the World Bank’s Indonesia Human Capital Acceleration multi-donor trust fund. This funding is directed towards strengthening JKN's capacity for implementation and coordination. From 2014 to 2019, JKN expanded its coverage dramatically, encompassing 83% of the population and significantly reducing individual out-of-pocket healthcare expenditures.

As of recent data, JKN covers approximately 220 million beneficiaries, with around 60% being from poor to near-poor communities. Despite this extensive coverage, there remains a substantial out-of-pocket (OOP) expenditure for healthcare in Indonesia, accounting for over 30% of the current health expenditure (CHE). The program's expansion and financial support have been pivotal in reducing these individual costs, yet the significant OOP spending highlights ongoing challenges in achieving complete healthcare financial security for all Indonesians.
