Introduction

This solution, Disaster and Climate Resilience Improvement Project (DCRIP) addresses natural catastrophe in Pakistan between Government of Pakistan, National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), Provincial Disaster Management Authorities (PDMAs), and World Bank (International Development Association - IDA). The program was implemented in 2015 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

The Disaster and Climate Resilience Improvement Project (DCRIP) is a World Bank-financed initiative aimed at reducing Pakistan's vulnerability to flooding and climate variability. The project focuses on restoring resilient flood protection infrastructure and strengthening government capacity for disaster risk management and climate variability. It aligns with Pakistan's disaster risk management framework and global climate adaptation priorities.

Key Highlights

The project features concessional IDA financing of US$125 million with a 25-year maturity and a 5-year grace period. It adopts an integrated approach combining infrastructure development and institutional capacity building. The project demonstrated operational agility during the COVID-19 pandemic by incorporating health-related procurement. It aligns with Pakistan's disaster risk management framework and climate adaptation agenda.

Results

The project was completed with an IDA Credit of SDR 88.9 million (US$125 million equivalent). It successfully restored flood protection infrastructure and strengthened institutional capacity. Pakistan's ranking as 8th on the Global Climate Risk Index in 2019 and its history of 26 major flood events since 1950 underscore the project's relevance. The 2010 floods alone affected 20 million people and caused nearly US$10 billion in losses.
