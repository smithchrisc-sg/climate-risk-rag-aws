Introduction

This solution, Pakistan Disaster Risk Management Program addresses natural catastrophe in Pakistan between National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), Provincial Disaster Management Authorities (PDMAs), Sindh Irrigation Department, Planning & Development Department (provincial), World Bank, Global Facility for Disaster Reduction and Recovery (GFDRR), and European Union (EU). The program was implemented in 2005 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The Pakistan Disaster Risk Management Program is a World Bank-led initiative aimed at enhancing Pakistan's disaster and climate resilience. It focuses on shifting the country's approach from reactive disaster response to proactive risk management. The program integrates technical assistance, investment projects, and policy support, working in collaboration with national and provincial disaster management authorities to strengthen institutional capacity, improve hydrometeorological services, and develop fiscal disaster risk financing strategies.

Key Highlights

The program is notable for its proactive risk management approach, integrating fiscal risk financing with disaster risk reduction efforts. It employs a multi-donor, multi-level governance design, coupling hydrometeorological upgrades with infrastructure restoration and community-based disaster risk management. Key projects include the Disaster and Climate Resilience Improvement Project and the Sindh Resilience Project, which focus on infrastructure resilience and institutional strengthening.

Results

The Balochistan project, with a budget of US$5 million, was completed with a 'Satisfactory' rating in June 2016. The program has conducted risk assessments for capitals, restored flood infrastructure, and strengthened institutional capacities in Punjab and northern districts, with ongoing efforts in Sindh.
