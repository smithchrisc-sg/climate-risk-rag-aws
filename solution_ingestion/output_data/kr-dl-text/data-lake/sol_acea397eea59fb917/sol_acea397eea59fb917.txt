Introduction

This solution, National Flood Mitigation Infrastructure Program (NFMIP) addresses natural catastrophe in Australia between Commonwealth of Australia, Department of Home Affairs, Federal Financial Relations, Queensland Reconstruction Authority (QRA), Tasmania State Emergency Service (SES), Government of South Australia, and Local governments and public asset owners. The program was implemented in 2020 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The National Flood Mitigation Infrastructure Program (NFMIP) is an Australian Government initiative aimed at reducing flood risks through capital grants for essential public infrastructure. Funded by the Commonwealth's Emergency Response Fund, the program co-invested with state, territory, and local governments to construct or upgrade levees, stormwater networks, and other flood mitigation structures. The program's objectives include reducing community flood risk, lowering future disaster recovery costs, and improving the insurability of infrastructure and communities.

Key Highlights

The NFMIP is notable for its $100 million ERF-backed capital injection focused exclusively on flood mitigation infrastructure. It aligns with the Disaster Recovery Funding Arrangements (DRFA) to reduce future eligible expenditure and improve insurance outcomes. The program's governance is supported by bilateral schedules under the Federal Financial Relations framework, ensuring legal clarity and intergovernmental cooperation. Localized prioritization allowed state agencies to engage councils in identifying and submitting locally-led priorities.

Results

The program funded numerous projects across Australia, with delivery continuing through to completion dates as late as June 2024. Specific projects include levee and channel works, drainage upgrades, and flood protection for high-consequence community assets, such as the South Hobart Primary School.
