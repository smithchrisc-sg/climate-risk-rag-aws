Introduction

This solution, Fourth Disaster Risk Management Development Policy Loan with a Catastrophe-Deferred Drawdown Option (CAT-DDO 4) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Philippines between Government of the Republic of the Philippines, Local Government Units (LGUs), World Bank (International Bank for Reconstruction and Development, IBRD), International reinsurance and investors, and Microinsurance markets. The program was implemented in 2021 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The CAT-DDO 4 is a World Bank-supported contingent credit line that provides the Philippines with rapid access to liquidity in the event of a disaster or public health emergency. It is part of the country's broader disaster risk management and financing strategy, designed to strengthen institutional and financial capacity to manage risks from climate change, natural disasters, and disease outbreaks.

Key Highlights

Immediate liquidity under public emergency declarations: Enables rapid budget mobilization during response and early recovery., 
Long availability and renewability: Initial three-year availability with renewals up to fifteen years., 
Policy-linked design: Incentivizes and supports institutional reforms in disaster risk management and health emergency management., 
Complements risk transfer: Positioned alongside insurance and ILS instruments to optimize cost and coverage across risk layers., 
Focus on protecting the vulnerable and fiscal health: Cushions vulnerable households and prevents disaster-induced poverty traps.

Results

2018 drawdown after Typhoon Mangkhut: Nearly US$500 million drawn to support recovery., 
Strengthened policy and institutional framework: Enhanced technical and financial capacity to manage climate, disaster, and disease risks.
