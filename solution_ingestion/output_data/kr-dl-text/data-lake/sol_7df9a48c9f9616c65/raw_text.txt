Introduction

This solution, Fourth Disaster Risk Management Development Policy Loan with a Catastrophe-Deferred Drawdown Option (CAT-DDO 4) addresses natural catastrophe in Republic of the Philippines between Government of the Philippines, International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD), World Bank, Japan–World Bank Program for Mainstreaming Disaster Risk Management in Developing Countries, and Global Facility for Disaster Reduction and Recovery (GFDRR). The program was implemented in 2021 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The Fourth Disaster Risk Management Development Policy Loan with a Catastrophe-Deferred Drawdown Option (CAT-DDO 4) is a $500 million contingent credit line from the World Bank, designed to strengthen the Philippines' capacity to manage climate, disaster, and health-related risks. It provides rapid access to liquidity following a national State of Calamity or Public Health Emergency declaration, supporting policy and institutional reforms for disaster risk management and public health emergency readiness.

Key Highlights

CAT-DDO 4 is the fourth such operation in the Philippines, featuring a unique public health emergency trigger. It is part of a broader disaster risk financing strategy that includes parametric insurance, reinsurance for public assets, microinsurance, and a catastrophe bond. The credit line is renewable for up to 15 years, ensuring sustained protection and reform momentum. It also integrates capacity-building initiatives like the Ready to Rebuild program and LGU COVID-19 recovery guide.

Results

The CAT-DDO 4 was disbursed after Typhoon Rai in December 2021, providing immediate liquidity. Previous CAT-DDOs were successfully drawn for Tropical Storm Washi (2011), Typhoon Mangkhut (2018), and the COVID-19 pandemic. A formal Implementation Completion and Results Report was finalized in April 2025.
