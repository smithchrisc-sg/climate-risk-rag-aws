Introduction

This solution, Start Network Disaster Risk Financing (DRF) System in Pakistan addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Pakistan between National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), Start Network, UK’s Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO), Government of the Netherlands, University of Reading, and London School of Economics (LSE). The program was implemented in 2017 and focuses on collaboration and leveraging tech.

Description

The Start Network Disaster Risk Financing (DRF) System in Pakistan is a multi-hazard initiative designed to address climate-related risks such as droughts, floods, and heatwaves. It combines risk modeling, anticipatory planning, and pre-arranged finance to enable proactive humanitarian action. Developed in collaboration with national authorities and technical partners, the system uses pre-agreed triggers to release funds before impacts escalate, aiming to protect vulnerable communities more effectively than traditional response models.

Key Highlights

The DRF system is science-led and integrates distinct parametric models tailored to drought, heatwave, and flood dynamics. It focuses on under-monitored seasons, such as winter agricultural droughts, and operates in urban centers to mitigate heatwave impacts. The system is embedded with national systems, built in collaboration with NDMA, and linked to pooled risk financing through Start Ready, enhancing sustainability and predictability.

Results

The system has been operationalized for drought and heatwaves and applied for floods in 2025 using both ex-ante and ex-post instruments. Technical model outputs have been used by non-member organizations, indicating broader sectoral influence. However, specific quantified impact metrics are not publicly available.
