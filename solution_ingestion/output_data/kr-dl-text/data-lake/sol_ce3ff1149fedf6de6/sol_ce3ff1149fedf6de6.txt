Introduction

This solution, Consolidation and Mainstreaming of Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) in China addresses natural catastrophe in China between Government of China, Integrated Research on Disaster Risk (IRDR), United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO), Global Facility for Disaster Reduction and Recovery (GFDRR), and World Bank. The program was implemented in 1998 and focuses on collaboration, regulation, and raising awareness.

Description

The Consolidation and Mainstreaming of Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) in China is a national policy initiative embedded in the country's Five-Year Plan system and Comprehensive Disaster Prevention and Mitigation Plans. It aims to integrate DRR into development planning, standardize disaster data systems, and orient governance towards prevention, preparedness, and risk management in alignment with the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction (2015–2030). The initiative focuses on reducing disaster mortality and economic losses, enhancing monitoring and information platforms, and utilizing risk financing tools adapted to local contexts.

Key Highlights

The initiative is characterized by its integration into China's Five-Year Plans, emphasizing a strategic shift from relief to prevention, and from single-hazard to multi-hazard management. It includes standardized disaster loss statistics, significantly improving post-disaster loss estimation speed and quality. International cooperation, such as the IRDR-UNESCO sub-forum, promotes multi-stakeholder collaboration on climate-related vulnerabilities, culture, and public health.

Results

The initiative has institutionalized DRR through five-year cycles since 2007, with significant improvements in disaster loss estimation, reducing assessment time from 112 days after the 2008 Wenchuan Earthquake to 27 days for the 2013 Lushan Earthquake.
