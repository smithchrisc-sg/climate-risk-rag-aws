Introduction

This solution, Site-specific Coastal Protection Studies addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Singapore between PUB, Singapore’s National Water Agency, National Climate Change Secretariat (NCCS), Building & Construction Authority (BCA), National Environment Agency (NEA), CPG Consultants, Centre of Excellence hosted at NUS with NTU, SUTD, SIT, and and A*STAR. The program was implemented in 2021 and focuses on collaboration, regulation, and raising awareness.

Description

The Site-specific Coastal Protection Studies in Singapore are a series of multi-year, place-based studies led by PUB, Singapore’s National Water Agency, aimed at designing tailored adaptation measures against sea-level rise and compound coastal–inland flood risks. These studies are part of a broader national coastal protection and climate adaptation program, which includes policy, regulation, standards development, research and development, and integrated flood modeling capabilities. The studies are progressively rolled out across different segments of Singapore's coastline, starting with the City–East Coast stretch.

Key Highlights

The initiative features multifunctional, adaptive designs and integrates nature-based solutions with engineering options. It employs a national Coastal–Inland Flood Model and is developing a new national code of practice. The program includes a Centre of Excellence with a S$125 million budget, aiming to create 40–50 research jobs and train 20–30 PhD students. The studies involve segmenting the coastline into hydraulically distinct zones, allowing for tailored protection measures.

Results

The initiative is ongoing, with significant milestones including the initiation of studies across multiple segments, development of national modeling capabilities, and the establishment of a Centre of Excellence. Approximately 70–80% of Singapore's coastline is already protected by hard defenses.
