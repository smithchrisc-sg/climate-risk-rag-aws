Introduction

This solution, Awanui River Flood Management Scheme addresses natural catastrophe in New Zealand between Northland Regional Council, Far North District Council, and New Zealand Government. The program was implemented in Early 1900s and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The Awanui River Flood Management Scheme is a comprehensive flood protection initiative in Northland, New Zealand, focusing on the Awanui River and Kaitāia urban area. Managed by the Northland Regional Council, the scheme has evolved from early tidal containment efforts to a modern program integrating structural upgrades, emergency bypasses, and ongoing maintenance. It aims to enhance floodwater conveyance, raise protection standards, and mitigate spill risks, supported by a region-wide funding model.

Key Highlights

The scheme includes a unique emergency spillway beneath Bell’s Hill slip to address landslide-induced blockage risks, a 15% increase in floodwater conveyance capacity, and a funding model that distributes costs 70% regionally and 30% locally. It employs drone-assisted surveying for asset management and aligns with Northland Regional Council's long-term infrastructure plans.

Results

The scheme aims to handle 15% more floodwater, protect urban Kaitāia against a 1-in-100-year flood, and surrounding areas against a 1-in-20-year flood. Accelerated by COVID-19 recovery funding, the project is set to complete in two years instead of six, reducing risk exposure and easing local ratepayer pressure.
