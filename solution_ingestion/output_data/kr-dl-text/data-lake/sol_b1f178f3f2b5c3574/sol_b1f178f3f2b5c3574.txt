Introduction

This solution, New Zealand Volcano Science Advisory Panel (NZVSAP) addresses natural catastrophe in New Zealand between National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA), GNS Science, University of Auckland, University of Canterbury, Massey University, GeoNet, New Zealand Defence Force (NZDF), Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade (MFAT), and Land Information New Zealand (LINZ). The program was implemented in 2008 and focuses on collaboration and raising awareness.

Description

The New Zealand Volcano Science Advisory Panel (NZVSAP) is a national advisory mechanism established to provide coordinated volcanic science advice during volcanic unrest and eruptions in New Zealand. Hosted by the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA), it integrates multi-institutional expertise to deliver real-time monitoring and scientific assessments to decision-makers, stakeholders, and the public. The panel is embedded within New Zealand's emergency management system and collaborates with GeoNet for data and monitoring.

Key Highlights

The NZVSAP is characterized by its rapid activation capability, multi-institutional and trans-disciplinary science integration, and consistent national messaging. It coordinates post-event research and provides open-data-enabled advice via GeoNet. The panel also engages in regional science diplomacy in the Pacific, supporting partners during major volcanic events.

Results

The panel has been activated during significant volcanic crises, including Te Maari (2012), Whakaari/White Island (2019), and Hunga Tonga–Hunga Ha’apai (2022). It has demonstrated rapid advice mobilization, providing assessments within hours for events like the Tonga eruption.
