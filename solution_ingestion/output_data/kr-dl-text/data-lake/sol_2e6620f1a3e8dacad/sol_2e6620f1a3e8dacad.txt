Introduction

This solution, Doubling of the Residential Building Cap and Levy Adjustment under EQCover (Toka Tū Ake EQC) addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in New Zealand between Natural Hazards Commission Toka Tū Ake, New Zealand Government, and Private home insurers. The program was implemented in 2022 and focuses on accessibility and collaboration.

Description

The initiative involves a recalibration of New Zealand's public natural hazard insurance scheme, EQCover, which doubled the residential building cap from NZD $150,000 to NZD $300,000 and reduced the levy rate from 20 cents to 16 cents per $100 of EQCover. This change aims to address rising house prices and construction costs, enhancing household financial resilience against natural hazards. The scheme is administered by the Natural Hazards Commission, Toka Tū Ake, and involves private insurers who cover losses above the EQCover cap.

Key Highlights

The initiative features automatic attachment of public catastrophe cover to private home insurance policies, a doubled cap aligned with cost inflation, and a levy rate reduction alongside the higher cap. It also includes coverage for certain residential land damage from storms and floods, ensuring broad participation and risk pooling.

Results

The building cap increased by 100% from $150,000 to $300,000, and the levy rate was reduced by 20% from 20 cents to 16 cents per $100 of EQCover. The maximum EQCover levy is now $480 for homes insured above $300,000. Most policyholders are expected to see higher total EQCover levies despite the lower rate due to the larger coverage base.
