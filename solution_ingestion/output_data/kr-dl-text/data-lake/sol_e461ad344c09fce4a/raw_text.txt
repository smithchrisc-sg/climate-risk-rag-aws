Introduction

This solution, Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) Funding Model Reform addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in New Zealand between Department of Internal Affairs (DIA), New Zealand Government/Cabinet, Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ), Insurance Council of New Zealand (ICNZ), and Property sector stakeholders. The program was implemented in 2026 and focuses on regulation and collaboration.

Description

The Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) Funding Model Reform is a legislative initiative aimed at refining the funding mechanism for New Zealand's national fire and emergency services. The reform, implemented via the Fire and Emergency New Zealand (Levy) Amendment Act 2023, transitions the funding model from a broad insurance-based levy to one more closely aligned with fire-risk insurance. This change is designed to ensure a stable, equitable, and predictable funding base for FENZ, which is responsible for responding to fires, hazardous substances incidents, and transport accidents.

Key Highlights

The reform narrows the levy to apply only to fire-damage insurance policies, aligning the funding mechanism with the specific peril FENZ addresses. It also simplifies the levy calculation by moving from 'amount insured' to 'sum insured', reflecting modern insurance contract structures. The reform process involved extensive consultation with stakeholders, including the insurance and property sectors, and is guided by principles of stability, equity, and predictability.

Results

The legislative changes have been enacted, with the new levy settings scheduled to commence on 1 July 2026. The reform aims to provide a more sustainable funding model for FENZ, ensuring its capacity to respond to emergencies effectively.
