Introduction

This solution, Guidelines for Natural Hazard Risk Analysis on Public Conservation Lands and Waters addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in New Zealand between Department of Conservation (DOC), GNS Science, and TTAC Ltd. The program was implemented in 2024 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The Guidelines for Natural Hazard Risk Analysis on Public Conservation Lands and Waters, developed by the Department of Conservation (DOC) in collaboration with GNS Science, provide a standardized methodology for assessing and managing natural hazard risks on public conservation lands in New Zealand. The guidelines focus on risks such as landslides, tsunamis, and volcanic activities, and aim to ensure visitor safety through a structured, quantitative risk analysis approach.

Key Highlights

The guidelines feature a multi-hazard standardization approach, quantitative risk thresholds aligned with visitor experience categories, and a three-tiered assessment pathway. They incorporate the ALARP principle for decision-making and involve an independent, multidisciplinary expert panel for reviews. The methodology is publicly accessible and is designed to be used for new developments, changes in site use or hazard, and ongoing risk locations.

Results

The guidelines have led to consistent and defensible decision-making across DOC visitor sites, with increased focus on landslide and rockfall hazards. While specific quantifiable outcomes are not detailed, the methodology has been applied in site casework, such as the Rob Roy Suspension Bridge assessment.
