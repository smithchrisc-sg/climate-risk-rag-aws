Description

Aotearoa New Zealand’s first National Adaptation Plan (NAP) is a comprehensive strategy to address the irreversible impacts of climate change. Launched in August 2022, it consolidates current and future government actions into a six-year program aimed at minimizing climate-related damage and building resilience across sectors and communities. The plan emphasizes protection of homes and buildings, urban planning to reduce risk exposure, and strengthening Māori connections to land and cultural knowledge. It is monitored by the Climate Change Commission for effectiveness.

Key Highlights

The NAP is New Zealand's first national adaptation plan, featuring bilingual framing and a focus on Māori partnerships and mātauranga. It aligns with the National Climate Change Risk Assessment and is independently monitored by the Climate Change Commission. The plan includes sectoral objectives for homes, buildings, and places, and emphasizes a coordinated national approach to adaptation.

Results

The NAP has established a national six-year adaptation work program with sectoral objectives for homes, buildings, and places. The Climate Change Commission published its first progress report in August 2024, but detailed effectiveness and outcome metrics are still under review.
