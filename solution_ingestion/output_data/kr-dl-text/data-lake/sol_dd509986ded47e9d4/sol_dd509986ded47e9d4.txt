Treasury

Introduction

This solution, Insurance Affordability and Natural Hazards Risk Reduction Taskforce addresses natural catastrophe through public private partnerships in Australia between Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet (PM&C), National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA), and Treasury. The program was implemented in 2024 and focuses on collaboration and regulation.

Description

The Insurance Affordability and Natural Hazards Risk Reduction Taskforce is a federal initiative in Australia aimed at addressing the challenges of rising natural hazard risks and increasing insurance costs. Led by the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, the Taskforce coordinates policy, standardizes hazard definitions, aligns data and mapping efforts, and seeks near-term affordability solutions in collaboration with other government agencies and the insurance sector. It complements existing national initiatives like the Hazards Insurance Partnership and the Cyclone Reinsurance Pool, focusing on reducing disaster risk at the community level and improving insurance affordability and availability across Australia.

Key Highlights

The Taskforce is led by the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, providing a whole-of-economy perspective. It focuses on standardizing hazard definitions, integrating data and mapping efforts, and coordinating with existing initiatives like the Hazards Insurance Partnership and the Cyclone Reinsurance Pool. The Taskforce aims to address both risk reduction and affordability, linking to national risk mapping and profiling efforts.

Results

The Taskforce is in its early stages, with formal launch and scope defined. Quantifiable sector context includes budget allocations for related initiatives, such as A$22.6 million over four years for the Hazards Insurance Partnership and A$24.3 million over five years for affordability initiatives. The Cyclone Reinsurance Pool is backed by up to A$10 billion per annum, with large insurers participating by late 2024. Taskforce-specific measured outcomes have not yet been publicly reported.
